How are points valued?
Cash Value (1¢/pt)

Every point is worth exactly 1¢. This is what you get if you redeem points as a statement credit, direct deposit, or gift card. Simple and guaranteed.

Transfer Value (varies by currency)

Points can be transferred to airline and hotel partners (like Hyatt, United, Delta) where they're often worth more. Transfer valuations are based on industry-standard estimates:

Chase Ultimate Rewards1.5¢/pt
Amex Membership Rewards1.5¢/pt
Citi ThankYou Points1.5¢/pt
Bilt Points1.5¢/pt
Capital One Miles1¢/mi
All other currencies1¢/pt

Transfer values are estimates. Actual value depends on how and when you redeem.

How it works. Tell us roughly what you spend each month in a few categories, like dining, groceries and gas. For every card, we add up the rewards you'd earn at that card's rates, add the statement credits you'd realistically use, and subtract the annual fee. The result is one number: what the card would put in your pocket over a year. Cards are ranked by that number, so the card at the top is the one that pays you the most for the way you already spend. Ranks 4 and 5 are free to see. The top 3, the full list and the wallet builder are a one-time $19 unlock. No signup, and no bank pays us for placement.

How we turn points into dollars. Cash back counts at face value. Points and miles count at what they're typically worth when moved to airline and hotel partners: Chase, Amex, Citi and Bilt points at 1.5¢ each, Capital One miles at 1¢, and hotel points lower. Prefer the simple view? Switch to Cash Value above the list to count every point at 1¢. Real value depends on how you redeem, so treat these numbers as a well-informed estimate rather than a promise. Not financial advice.

Where a card can only be held with a paid membership, like Costco or Robinhood Gold, we count that cost against the card too.

* Why our credit values look lower than the bank's. Issuers advertise statement credits at face value. Most people never use them in full: a $15 monthly Uber credit is worth nothing in a month you don't ride, and a hotel credit that only works through one booking site is worth less than cash. So we discount each credit by how easy it is to use: automatic 100%, flexible 90%, monthly expiring 60%, portal-only 50%, single-merchant 30%.
